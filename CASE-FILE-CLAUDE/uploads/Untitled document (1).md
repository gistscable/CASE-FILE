**OPENING HOOK**

Just after 3:30 p.m. on April 17, 2024, Tampa police responded to reports of gunfire near railroad tracks between Busch Boulevard and Rome Avenue. There they found fourteen-year-old Jevario Buie. He died from his injuries.

Jevario’s family had been looking forward to his eighth-grade graduation. In the days after his death, they asked the community for information and created a place for people to share tips.

Police released video of Jevario walking with two other people before the shooting and asked for help identifying them. Later, police said other footage showed him with one of those people near the tracks. Moments afterward, that person was seen walking away without Jevario.

It took nearly eight months before an arrest was made. Four days after police arrested fifteen-year-old Jahmir Moore, he escaped from the juvenile detention center where he was being held.

At trial, the accounts of what happened between the teenagers would differ. Prosecutors argued that the killing was deliberate. The defense said Moore acted in self-defense.

**CHAPTER 1 — JEVARIO AND THE PEOPLE AROUND HIM**

Jevario Buie was fourteen and in the eighth grade at Memorial Middle School in Tampa. His family remembered a playful teenager who enjoyed football and video games. He was looking ahead to high school and hoped to attend Tampa Bay Technical High School. His grandmother, JoQuetta Winters, recalled him working on the application and asking his mother for help with an essay. His family said he hoped to study welding and ironwork and possibly play football there. He was also looking forward to learning to drive.

His family had been preparing for his middle school graduation that spring. His mother, Jasmine Baldwin, said he loved his family and protected his sisters. His grandfather, Michael Baldwin Sr., described a family that included three sisters, an aunt and cousins. After Jevario’s death, his grandparents shared memories of him and urged the Tampa community to help investigators find out what had happened.

Jahmir Moore was fifteen when Jevario was killed. Police would later identify Moore as one of the teenagers seen with him that afternoon.

**CHAPTER 2 — THE MEETING**

After the shooting, police said investigators found records that Jahmir Moore and Jevario Buie had communicated before Jevario’s death.

At Moore’s trial, prosecutors argued that a dispute involving teenagers preceded the shooting and that Moore had acted deliberately. His defense argued that the shooting was not planned and that Moore had acted in self-defense. Those were the competing accounts presented in court.

According to trial reporting, Moore had arranged to meet another teenager on April 17, and Jevario asked to join them. The three spent time together that afternoon. Surveillance footage would later show parts of their movements.

**CHAPTER 3 — APRIL 17**

On Wednesday, April 17, 2024, the three teenagers traveled together in Tampa’s Lowry Park neighborhood. Police obtained footage from nearby residences. According to Tampa police, video showed Jevario, Moore and another person walking together between 2:41 and 3:27 p.m.

Trial reporting said the three met at an abandoned building. The third teenager later left, and Jevario continued with Moore.

At 3:33 p.m., police said, Jevario and Moore were filmed walking north on North Rome Avenue toward a dead end near the tracks. At 3:36, Moore was seen walking away without Jevario. Police said he was moving west and south from the area. The footage gave detectives separate views of the afternoon, not a continuous recording of every movement.

Police reported that officers responded to calls about gunfire shortly after 3:30 p.m. They found Jevario near the railroad tracks between Busch Boulevard and Rome Avenue. He died from his injuries.

The surrounding area became the scene of a homicide investigation.

**CHAPTER 4 — A FAMILY’S APPEAL**

Jasmine Baldwin said police told her of her son’s death about six hours later. On April 22, Tampa police publicly appealed for information. Jevario’s family authorized police to release photographs to help generate leads, while also requesting privacy. His grandparents, Michael Baldwin Sr. and JoQuetta Winters, spoke publicly as the family sought answers. At that point, no arrest had been announced.

His family launched a website sharing Jevario’s story and inviting people to submit information anonymously. After a community vigil, they gathered on May 4 for his funeral at Bible-Based Fellowship Church of Temple Terrace. State Attorney Suzy Lopez and members of Moms Demand Action and Everytown for Gun Safety attended. Relatives wore green, his favorite color.

On May 9, police released a short home surveillance video showing Jevario with two other people near West Busch Boulevard about ten minutes before the shooting. Their faces were not visible. Chief Lee Bercaw called the companions persons of interest. Detectives asked the public to help identify them.

Alongside the video came a larger reward. The ATF’s Tampa Field Division, Crime Stoppers of Tampa Bay, the Florida Attorney General’s Office and community partners offered up to twenty thousand dollars for information leading to the identification and arrest of those responsible. The family had also contributed to an earlier reward.

Among the community partners was Thaddeus M. Bullard, known as WWE wrestler Titus O’Neil. Bullard said students at a school bearing his name knew Jevario and his family, while staff had worked with Jevario. He urged the community to help.

**CHAPTER 5 — WHAT SURVEILLANCE SHOWED**

Tampa police gathered surveillance from the area around West Waters Avenue, West Busch Boulevard and North Rome Avenue. The recordings included video from nearby homes. Each camera captured a separate view, giving detectives sightings they could place in order.

Police said the footage showed Jevario walking with two other people that afternoon. Another view showed him with Moore near North Rome Avenue. A later view showed Moore leaving the area without Jevario.

The recordings gave investigators a timeline, but being seen with someone shortly before a killing does not by itself establish who fired a gun. The images had to be considered alongside other evidence.

When police released the video in May, they asked the public to help identify the two people seen with Jevario. In December, police identified Moore as one of them. Investigators also found records of communications between Moore and Jevario before the shooting. Those records gave detectives another part of the investigation to examine.

**CHAPTER 6 — THE DIGITAL EVIDENCE**

Tampa police said their investigation drew on more than surveillance video. Detectives reported finding records of Jahmir Moore communicating with Jevario, along with social media messages, notes and search history. Police said the material implicated Moore in Jevario’s death.

Police also said one social media message contained a detail about the killing that had not been publicly released. Their announcement did not disclose the detail.

At trial, prosecutors presented messages and social media posts alongside the surveillance footage. Moore’s defense disputed the prosecution’s interpretation of the digital material. The evidence was considered as part of the wider case, not just as a single message or recording.

On December 4, police took Moore into custody in connection with Jevario’s death.

**CHAPTER 7 — ARREST AND ADULT PROSECUTION**

On December 4, 2024, the Tampa Police Department, working with the U.S. Marshals Task Force, arrested fifteen-year-old Jahmir Moore in connection with Jevario Buie’s shooting death. It had been 231 days since April 17\. Police made the arrest public the following day and announced a first-degree murder charge.

In a police release, State Attorney Suzy Lopez said Moore would be prosecuted as an adult. The announcement did not establish guilt; the case would proceed to court.

Jevario’s mother, Jasmine Baldwin, and grandfather, Michael Baldwin Sr., thanked investigators. Jasmine said learning a suspect’s name brought some closure, but she still wanted to know why. Michael expressed sadness that another teenager stood accused.

**CHAPTER 8 — ESCAPE, SEARCH AND RECAPTURE**

Four days after his arrest, Jahmir Moore was being held at the Hillsborough Regional Juvenile Detention Center, in the custody of Florida’s Department of Juvenile Justice. Police said he was in a fenced outdoor recreation area on the morning of Sunday, December 8, 2024\. A subsequent headcount showed he had not returned inside.

A nearby business provided video showing Moore running away from the facility at about 10:45 a.m. Tampa police said they began searching as soon as they were notified. Deputy Chief Ruth Cate described a response that included canine teams, aviation, uniformed officers and plainclothes investigators. Officers reviewed video from nearby businesses and asked anyone who saw Moore to call 911 rather than approach him.

The search unfolded near Raymond James Stadium, where the Buccaneers were playing the Raiders. Major Patrick Messmer said Tampa police had a strong presence at the stadium, supported by the Hillsborough County Sheriff’s Office and Florida Highway Patrol.

Messmer said detectives drew on information gathered while locating Moore before his first arrest. They watched several people who knew him, and that surveillance led them to a residence. Shortly after 9:15 p.m., Tampa police, assisted by the U.S. Marshals Task Force, arrested Moore without incident. Cate said he was in his parents’ company when he was found. Messmer said the residence was not his parents’ home, and police said his parents were cooperating.

Police announced an additional felony escape charge. Cate said Moore was transferred to a juvenile facility in Pinellas County, where he was not in general population.

Jevario’s grandfather, Michael Baldwin Sr., said he feared for his family’s safety and questioned how Moore had escaped. His grandmother, JoQuetta Winters, said police kept the family informed and sent officers to relatives’ homes. The Department of Juvenile Justice announced an investigation into the escape.

With the search over, the murder case continued. Prosecutors would soon ask a judge to keep Moore in jail while he waited for trial.

**CHAPTER 9 — PRETRIAL DETENTION**

On December 16, 2024, Assistant State Attorney Chinwe Fossett filed a written request for pretrial detention, asking that Jahmir Moore remain jailed while awaiting trial. The Tampa Bay Times reported that the request described an investigation using surveillance video, social media and cellphone records.

The next morning, Moore appeared in Hillsborough County Circuit Court. His attorney did not contest the request. Judge J. Logan Murphy ordered the fifteen-year-old held without bail in the murder case. The order concerned pretrial custody, not guilt. Moore was already being held in an adult jail. State Attorney Suzy Lopez said he would be housed at the Hillsborough County Jail to protect the community and prevent another escape.

On September 1, 2026, Moore was seventeen. Jury selection began in Tampa, followed by opening statements. The jury would hear competing accounts of Jevario’s death.

**CHAPTER 10 — THE TRIAL**

At trial, Assistant State Attorney Chinwe Fossett argued that Moore had deliberately killed Jevario. She said the shooting was connected to a dispute involving other teenagers. Defense attorney Sinardi offered another explanation. He argued that Moore acted in self-defense and that the shooting was not planned in advance.

Prosecutors showed jurors surveillance footage, messages and social media posts. They argued that the material supported their case. Sinardi disputed the prosecution’s interpretation of his client’s posts and messages while acknowledging that they were inappropriate.

A third teenager testified as a prosecution witness. An associate medical examiner also testified about Jevario’s injuries, according to the Tampa Bay Times.

Moore took the witness stand. He admitted that he shot Jevario, but said he acted in self-defense during a struggle over a gun. During cross-examination, Fossett questioned him about his account and his messages. Moore disputed the prosecution’s characterization of many of them.

On September 3, 2026, the jury found Moore guilty of first-degree murder. Jevario’s mother, Jasmine Baldwin, had watched nearly all of the trial. His grandfather, Michael Baldwin Sr., said the family believed the jury got it right while recognizing that no verdict could undo their loss. Sentencing followed the next month.

**CHAPTER 11 — SENTENCING**

On October 16, 2026, Jahmir Moore returned to a Hillsborough County courtroom for sentencing. He was seventeen, and he had been fifteen when he killed Jevario. He faced a sentence of forty years to life in prison.

Assistant State Attorney Chinwe Fossett asked Judge Lyann Goudie for life in prison. She argued the killing was deliberate and that Moore had shown little remorse, pointing to his posts after Jevario’s death and his escape from detention. Jevario’s family also asked the court for life. His mother, sister and grandparents spoke about the loss of future birthdays, holidays and family gatherings with him. His grandmother, JoQuetta Winters, held up his photograph while addressing Moore. His grandfather, Michael Baldwin Sr., urged Moore to acknowledge the harm he had caused and change.

Defense attorney Sinardi argued that Moore’s life experiences suggested the possibility of future reform. Moore’s mother, Tabia Aska, spoke of a funny, playful son who loved his family and playing football. She asked the judge not to see him as a monster and expressed sympathy to Jevario’s relatives. According to the Tampa Bay Times, Moore offered condolences and an apology.

Judge Goudie recognized the seriousness of the killing and the lasting harm to Jevario’s family. She also considered Moore’s age and immaturity when deciding not to impose a life sentence.

Goudie sentenced Moore to fifty-five years in prison for the murder.

WFLA reported that the State Attorney’s Office shared two memories: Jevario asked his mother to watch his bicycle wheelies, and his grandfather recalled their races. For a killing committed at fifteen, how should a sentence balance accountability and the possibility of rehabilitation?

**CHAPTER 12 — REMEMBERING JEVARIO**

After sentencing, Jevario’s grandfather, Michael Baldwin Sr., said the proceeding had closed an important chapter, but it did not bring closure to the family’s loss.

Jevario’s family had looked forward to celebrating his eighth-grade graduation. He had hoped to attend Tampa Bay Tech and study welding. His mother, Jasmine Baldwin, and his relatives kept his name in public view as they sought answers and attended court hearings.
